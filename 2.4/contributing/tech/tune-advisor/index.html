    Tune Advisor Internals¶

    How the Tune Advisor works, end to end: what the flight controller measures, how the radio stores and combines it, the exact rules that produce the advice, and exactly which parameters Save writes. For using it, see Tune Advisor.

    The work is split in two:

    Side Does Source
    Flight controller Measures how the heli answers the rate loop and reports the numbers over MSP. Gives no advice and changes no settings. src/main/flight/tune_advisor.c, MSP2_GET_TUNE_ADVISOR in src/main/msp/msp.c (rotorflight-firmware)
    Radio (Ethos Lua suite) Reads the numbers after each flight, keeps the last 5 flights, works out the advice, and writes it to the flight controller when the pilot presses Save. tasks/tune_history.lua, lib/tune_history.lua, lib/msp_tune_advisor.lua, app/pages/tune_advisor.lua (rotorflight-lua-ethos-suite)

    The advice rules live on the radio so they can change without flashing the flight controller.

    Firmware: what is measured¶

    The feature is built when USE_TUNE_ADVISOR is defined (on by default in common_pre.h). tuneAdvisorUpdate() runs every PID loop, straight after pidController().

    Sampling¶

    PID loops are averaged into one sample every 10 ms (100 Hz). Each sample holds, per axis (roll, pitch, yaw):

    Value Taken from
    Setpoint (deg/s) The PID controller's setpoint
    Gyro (deg/s) The PID controller's gyro rate
    I-term The PID controller's I output
    Feedback P + I + D + B output: the PID sum minus F and the offset (O). F and collective-to-pitch precomp are left out so they do not count as braking.
    Stick deflection -1 to 1
    Saturated Set if the mixer saturated that axis in any loop of the block

    plus |collective| (0 to 1) from the stabilised collective mixer input.

    Which samples count¶

    A sample is valid only if, in every PID loop of its block:

    • the heli is armed, spooled up (governor) and airborne, and
    • none of Angle, Horizon, Trainer, Altitude hold, Rescue, GPS rescue or failsafe is active.

    On top of that, the body must have been rotating: |roll| + |pitch| + |yaw| rate of at least 40 deg/s within the last 200 samples (2 s). Yaw counts, so a pirouette is flying. This keeps spooled-up stick checks on the ground out.

    Invalid samples are not used for anything below. The reported flight time is the number of valid samples / 100, in seconds.

    Response (feed-forward match)¶

    F sends the setpoint straight to the servos, so the ratio gyro / setpoint says how well F fits the airframe: 1.0 means the heli flies the rate it is asked for; above 1 it turns faster than asked (F too high for the rates).

    The heli answers late, so the setpoint is compared delayed by 0 to 25 samples (0 to 250 ms). For each delay the firmware keeps three running sums over usable pairs: Σ setpoint·gyro, Σ setpoint², Σ gyro². A pair is usable when:

    • the current sample is valid and the axis is not saturated now, and
    • the delayed setpoint came from a valid, unsaturated sample with 40 ≤ |setpoint| < 200 deg/s.

    From these, for each delay:

    • gain = Σ setpoint·gyro / Σ setpoint² (least-squares slope through zero)
    • correlation = Σ setpoint·gyro / √(Σ setpoint² · Σ gyro²)

    Once a second the delay with the highest correlation becomes the best lag. The reported ffGain, ffCorr and ffCount are those of the best lag, and ffLagMs is the lag × 10 ms.

    At the best lag the same ratio is also split into bands:

    Bands Split by Edges
    Setpoint bands Size of the request, |setpoint| 40–100, 100–200, 200+ deg/s
    Collective bands |collective| below 25%, 25–50%, 50% and above (only requests below 200 deg/s)

    Full stick¶

    A sample counts as full stick when the stick deflection one best-lag ago was at least 90%. For those samples the firmware counts how many there were (fullCount) and how many were saturated (fullSatCount), and keeps the rate reached in the stick's direction against the rate asked:

    • fullRatio = Σ rate reached / Σ |setpoint|
    • fullMaxRate = the highest rate reached (deg/s)

    Saturated samples are included here: this is what tells the radio the cyclic ran out of travel.

    Stops (stick releases)¶

    Per axis, a small state machine follows each stick input:

    1. Input: starts when |setpoint| leaves the centre band (5 deg/s). While the stick is out, the setpoint and gyro extremes are kept.
    2. Release: when the setpoint returns inside 5 deg/s. The direction of the input is the side with the larger setpoint peak. At that moment the I-term is recorded, signed so that positive means opposing the input that just ended.
    3. Watch: for the next 80 samples (0.8 s), the largest rate in the opposite direction is the rebound, and the largest opposite feedback output is the counter-output. The rate peak in the input direction keeps updating for the first 10 samples, as the heli coasts.
    4. The release counts only if the input asked for at least 60 deg/s and the heli reached at least 60 deg/s. If the stick moves out again within 15 samples (0.15 s), it is not a stop and is dropped; after that, a new input ends the watch early and the release still counts.

    For each counted release the firmware adds up:

    Value Meaning
    releases Number of counted releases
    bigRebounds Releases with a rebound of 15% of the peak rate or more
    meanRebound Mean of rebound / peak rate
    meanOvershoot Mean of peak rate / peak setpoint
    meanCounter Mean peak counter-output (P + I + D + B) after the release
    meanIterm Mean I-term at the release, positive = pushing back against the input

    A small counter-output with the I-term near zero points at the airframe rebounding on its own (not enough damping). A positive I-term points at the I-term pushing the heli back.

    Accumulation and reset¶

    Everything accumulates in RAM across flights until it is reset. It is not saved and is lost at power-off. It is reset when:

    • MSP2_CLEAR_TUNE_ADVISOR is received (the radio sends it after saving each flight), or
    • the heli arms on a different tune. At each arm the firmware hashes the current PID and rate profile numbers, every PID gain (P, I, D, F, B, O) of the PID profile, Iterm Relax Cutoff, PID mode, rate type, and the RC rates, expo and super rates of all axes. If the hash differs from the previous arm, the statistics start again.

    The tune is only checked at arming. A setting changed in flight (for example by an adjustment function) is not noticed until the next arm.

    MSP interface¶

    Command ID Direction Payload
    MSP2_GET_TUNE_ADVISOR 0x5F10 Read Request: U8 axis (0 roll, 1 pitch, 2 yaw). Any other length or axis is an error.
    MSP2_CLEAR_TUNE_ADVISOR 0x5F11 Write Empty. Resets all statistics.

    One axis per request keeps the reply (67 bytes) small enough for MSP over telemetry. Firmware without the feature answers with an MSP error.

    Reply, payload version 1. Ratios are signed 16-bit × 1000; counts saturate at 65535; all values little-endian.

    Offset Type Field
    0 U8 Payload version (1)
    1 U8 Collecting now (1 = the current sample is valid)
    2 U16 Seconds of valid flight
    4 U8 Axis
    5 U16 P gain (current PID profile)
    7 U16 F gain
    9 U16 B gain
    11 U8 Iterm Relax Cutoff for this axis
    12 U8 Rate type (current rate profile)
    13 U8 RC rate for this axis (raw)
    14 U8 Super rate for this axis (raw)
    15 U16 ffCount
    17 S16 ffGain × 1000
    19 S16 ffCorr × 1000
    21 U16 ffLagMs
    23 3 × (S16, U16) Setpoint bands 40–100, 100–200, 200+: gain × 1000, count
    35 3 × (S16, U16) Collective bands <25%, 25–50%, 50%+: gain × 1000, count
    47 U16 fullCount
    49 U16 fullSatCount
    51 S16 fullRatio × 1000
    53 U16 fullMaxRate (deg/s)
    55 U16 releases
    57 U16 bigRebounds
    59 S16 meanRebound × 1000
    61 S16 meanOvershoot × 1000
    63 S16 meanCounter × 1000
    65 S16 meanIterm × 1000

    The tune values at offsets 5–14 are the settings the numbers belong to, so a client can work out new values without reading the profiles first. The radio refuses a reply of any other length or version rather than guess at it.

    Radio: capturing each flight¶

    The background task captures every flight, whether or not the Tune Advisor page is open. It only runs when the flight controller reports firmware 4.7.0 or newer (the planned first release with the feature); until the version is known it sends nothing.

    On each disarm:

    1. Wait 5 s (the flight-stats sync goes first).
    2. Read roll, then pitch, then yaw, with 2 s between one answer and the next request. Over S.Port and FBUS each reply takes about 15 telemetry frames, so the gaps keep sensors updating.
    3. If a read fails, ask again for that axis only after 5 s; axes already read are kept. Each read is sent at most 3 times. After 3 link errors the capture stops until the next disarm. The flight controller still holds the numbers, so that disarm saves them.
    4. If the three replies report different flight times, the flight controller restarted or flew between reads, and the capture starts again.
    5. Save the three axes as one flight, unless the flight time is 0 (no valid flight) or the same as the last flight saved for this model (the previous clear never arrived and nothing new was flown).
    6. Send MSP2_CLEAR_TUNE_ADVISOR, so the next flight is measured on its own.

    Arming during a capture abandons it. The flight controller was not cleared, so on the same tune the next disarm saves both flights as one; on a changed tune the firmware resets at arming and the earlier flight is lost. A disarm during a link loss is captured on reconnect, as long as the flight controller was not powered off.

    Files on the radio¶

    All under LOGS:/rfsuite/tune/<mcuId>/, where <mcuId> is the flight controller's ID (the same one that names the flight log folders):

    File Contents
    history.csv One row per axis per flight, newest last. Only the last 5 flights are kept. Columns: date, flight_seconds, axis, then every reply field in the order above (p, f, b, relax_cutoff, rates_type, rc_rate, s_rate, ff_count, …, mean_iterm). A file with a different header is started again.
    logs.ini The model name.
    changes.csv Every value Save wrote: date,axis,setting,from,to, raw values as the flight controller stores them. Appended, never trimmed, never erased by the page.

    Combining flights¶

    For each axis separately, the page starts from the newest flight and goes back up to 5 flights, stopping at the first flight whose tune on that axis differs from the newest one. The tune is: P, F, B, Iterm Relax Cutoff, rate type, RC rate and super rate for that axis.

    • Counts are added (ffCount, band counts, fullCount, fullSatCount, releases, bigRebounds, seconds).
    • ffGain, ffCorr and ffLagMs are averaged weighted by ffCount; each band's gain by its count; fullRatio by fullCount; the four stop means by releases.
    • fullMaxRate is the highest of the flights.

    The firmware's gains are weighted by setpoint² within a flight, so the combined response is a close estimate. The stop figures combine exactly.

    Radio: the advice rules¶

    All rules work per axis, on the combined numbers. At most 3 changes and 3 reasons are shown; a change and its reason are added together or not at all.

    Thresholds¶

    Name Value Used for
    Response data needed ffCount ≥ 1000 (10 s) Judging the response at all
    Consistent enough ffCorr ≥ 0.85 Judging the response at all
    Too fast / too slow ffGain > 1.15 / < 0.85 F and rate change
    F step limit ±20% per step F change
    Full stick data needed fullCount ≥ 100 Full-stick rate change
    Cyclic at its limit fullSatCount ≥ 50% of fullCount Full-stick rate change
    Rate not reached fullMaxRate < 80% of the rate asked Full-stick rate change
    Stops needed releases ≥ 10 Judging stops at all
    Bounce too big meanRebound ≥ 0.12 Stop changes
    I-term pushing back meanIterm ≥ 0.03 Iterm Relax Cutoff change
    Collective fact both outer bands ≥ 300 samples, spread ≥ 25% Information only

    Response¶

    Checked in this order:

    1. ffCount < 1000: Needs more flying (shown as a percentage of 1000). The page asks for rolls, flips or pirouettes. No change.
    2. ffCorr < 0.85: Too uneven to judge. No change.

    3. ffGain above 1.15 or below 0.85, and F > 0: change F and the rates together:

      • new F = round(F × clamp(1 / ffGain, 0.8, 1.2)), limited to 1–1000
      • rate factor k = old F / new F
      • each rate value that scales the curve linearly (table below) becomes round(raw × k), limited to 1–255

      F × rate is what the pilot feels at the stick, so it stays the same; the setpoint now matches what the heli does. With F = 0 on the axis (common on yaw) there is no F advice: the PID gains set the response. 4. Otherwise the response matches. Then, on roll and pitch only, if the full-stick conditions above all hold, the rates are scaled by k = fullMaxRate / rate asked, so full stick asks for what the heli can reach.

    Rate values scaled, by rate type:

    Rate type Values scaled Max rate asked at full stick
    Actual RC rate (centre sensitivity) and super rate (max rate) max(RC rate, super rate) × 10 deg/s
    Quick RC rate and super rate (max rate) max(super rate × 10, RC rate × 2) deg/s
    Rotorflight RC rate only (super rate is the curve shape) RC rate × 5 deg/s
    Betaflight, Raceflight, KISS, None None: the page shows "all rates about N% higher/lower" Not known: no full-stick advice

    For the last group the curve cannot be scaled exactly, so the rate change is shown as a percentage only and not written. The F change that goes with it is not written either, because F alone would change the stick feel.

    Stops¶

    1. releases < 10: Needs more stops. No change.
    2. meanRebound < 0.12: shown as the bounce-back percentage. No change.
    3. meanRebound ≥ 0.12, first matching case:
      • I-term pushing back (meanIterm ≥ 0.03) and Iterm Relax Cutoff > 1, and room for a change: new cutoff = round(cutoff × 0.8), limited to 1 … (cutoff − 1). A lower cutoff relaxes the I-term more.
      • The response is still off (step 3 above applies) and F > 0: reason only, "fix F first".
      • Otherwise, with room for a change: new P = round(P × 1.2), limited to (P + 1) … 1000. The page also mentions B as an alternative but never changes B.

    Collective fact¶

    If the response is judged and both the low and high collective bands have at least 300 samples, the larger band gain / smaller band gain is computed. At 1.25 or more the page adds a reason such as "It turns 30% faster at high collective than at low". This is information only and is the first reason dropped when there is no room.

    Radio: what Save writes¶

    Save writes the changes for the axis on screen only. These are the only parameters it can ever change:

    Setting (as the page names it) CLI parameter MSP message, field Accepted range
    PIDs > axis > P roll_p_gain, pitch_p_gain, yaw_p_gain MSP_SET_PID_TUNING (202), <axis>_p 0–1000
    PIDs > axis > F roll_f_gain, pitch_f_gain, yaw_f_gain MSP_SET_PID_TUNING (202), <axis>_f 0–1000
    Advanced > PID Controller > Iterm Relax Cutoff > axis iterm_relax_cutoff (one value per axis) MSP_SET_PID_PROFILE (95), iterm_relax_cutoff_<0-2> 1–100
    Rates > axis > RC rate / centre sensitivity roll_rc_rate, pitch_rc_rate, yaw_rc_rate MSP_SET_RC_TUNING (204), rcRates_<1-3> 1–255
    Rates > axis > Rate / max rate roll_srate, pitch_srate, yaw_srate MSP_SET_RC_TUNING (204), rates_<1-3> 1–255

    All of them belong to the active PID or rate profile. Save never changes I, D, B, O, expo, rate type, collective rates, filters, governor or any other setting, and never touches another axis or profile.

    The write sequence¶

    1. Confirm. The dialog lists every change exactly as the page shows it.
    2. Read MSP_PID_TUNING (112), MSP_PID_PROFILE (94) and MSP_RC_TUNING (111). A failed read, or a reply too short to hold every field, stops here with nothing changed.
    3. Check, and stop with nothing changed if any of these fail:
      • the heli is disarmed;
      • the active PID and rate profile are the same as when Save was pressed;
      • P, F, B, Iterm Relax Cutoff, rate type, RC rate and super rate on that axis are exactly the values of the newest flight the advice came from (so a hand edit, a profile switch or advice already applied stops it);
      • every value about to change still holds its expected old value, and every new value is in range.
    4. Write only the messages that contain a change, in the order PID tuning, PID profile, RC tuning. Each is a full-message write: every other field is sent back exactly as it was read in step 2.
    5. Save with MSP_EEPROM_WRITE (250).
    6. Verify: read all three messages again and check each changed field holds its new value.
    7. Append each change to changes.csv.

    If a write fails, nothing has been saved to EEPROM, and restarting the flight controller undoes any part already sent. If the EEPROM save fails, the changes are active until the next restart.

    The saved flights are kept. Because the tune changed, the next flight starts a new combination on the page, and the firmware resets its statistics when it next arms.

    Clearing¶

    The page's clear button deletes history.csv for the model and, when the firmware supports it, sends MSP2_CLEAR_TUNE_ADVISOR. changes.csv and logs.ini are kept.
