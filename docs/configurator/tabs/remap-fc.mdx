# Remap FC

Remap FC lets you change which servos, motors, and other functions are connected to which pins on your flight controller — for example, swapping a tail servo output for a motorised tail, adding a buffer pack or onboard glow, or moving a function to a different pin. It's all done from the Configurator, without soldering, rewiring, or building custom firmware.

This is most useful if your board's factory pin layout doesn't quite match how you want to wire your helicopter, or if you're using a board that doesn't have a purpose built Rotorflight target.

## Reading your flight controller

Connect to your flight controller, open the Remap FC tab, and click **Read FC** to read its current configuration. Nothing is changed on your board — this just loads what's already there.

Once the read finishes you'll see your board's name and MCU, a diagram of the board, and the pin table.

### Supported boards

The remapper covers the STM32F411, STM32F40X, STM32F7X2, STM32F745, STM32H743 and STM32G47X families, which should include every Rotorflight compatible MCU. If yours isn't supported, or couldn't be identified, Remap FC shows a **Board not supported** message instead of the table.

## The pin table

The table has two connected columns:

* **FC Label** – every physical connector on your board.
* **Feature** – every motor, servo, frequency input, and LED strip function currently in use. A connector with nothing assigned to it has no entry here.

A line connects each Feature to the pin it's currently wired to, so you can see the wiring at a glance.

Click an **FC Label** entry to open a card showing its pin number, a description, and the dropdown used to reassign it. Click a **Feature** entry instead and the card opens read-only, describing what that function is for. A few pins can't be reassigned at all — either they're hard-wired to something onboard, or they have no timer available — and their card says so instead of offering a dropdown.

Use **Other Pins** below the FC Label column to bring in a connector that isn't currently shown, then choose what you want it to do.

Turn on **Show details** if you want to see the calculated timer and DMA allocation for every function, along with the exact commands that will be sent to your flight controller. **Note: Most people won't need this. It is for information only**.

## Reassigning a function

To change what a pin does, click its entry in the **FC Label** column to open its card, then pick a different function from the dropdown. The function that was previously there is freed up, and picking **None** leaves the pin unassigned.

A few ordering rules apply, matching what the firmware can actually drive:

* Motors and servos have to be filled in sequence — Servo 4 can't be assigned until Servos 1, 2 and 3 all are.
* An output frequency input can only be assigned once its matching motor is — Freq 2 needs Motor 2.
* Rotorflight drives at most 4 motors and 8 servos, so higher numbered slots are never offered.

A connector that's normally a fixed serial (UART) or I2C port — such as SBUS or TLM — can be given a motor, servo, frequency input, or LED strip like any other pin, and its own original function can always be put back from its card. The one thing it can't do is take on a *different* serial or I2C function.

None of your changes take effect immediately — you can make as many edits as you like and review everything before applying them.

**Servo frequency groups**, below the pin card, lists each servo's update rate alongside the others sharing its timer. Servos on the same timer must always run at the same rate, so it's worth a glance after moving one to a new pin.

## Resolving pin conflicts

Not every pin can be freely reassigned to every function — some pins share internal hardware that can only be used one way at a time. Most of the time, Remap FC works this out automatically as you make changes.

If a change you've made can't work, you'll see a **Pin selection not valid** warning explaining the problem. Where possible, Remap FC offers a one click fix via **Accept Suggestion** — for example swapping two functions between pins, or moving a function to a free pin. If there's more than one way out, choose the one you want from the dropdown first. If it can't find an automatic fix, use **Reset to Set Option** to clear the affected function back to unassigned and pick a different pin for it yourself.

You won't be able to apply your changes until the conflict is resolved.

### Gaps in motor/servo numbering

You can still leave a hole in the numbering from above — freeing Servo 5 while Servo 6 stays put, for example. Remap FC flags this with a **Gap in motor/servo numbering** warning and blocks applying your changes, because the firmware stops at the first unassigned output rather than skipping it: a hole at Servo 5 would silently cost you Servo 5 and everything above it. Either assign the missing output, or move the higher ones down to close the gap.

## Applying your changes

Once you have unsaved changes, the usual toolbar appears at the bottom of the page:

* **Save and Reboot** sends your changes to the flight controller and saves them. This also **reboots your flight controller**, which is required for the new wiring to take effect. It's disabled while any pin conflict or numbering gap is outstanding.
* **Revert** discards everything you've changed and goes back to what was last read or applied.

Turn on **Show details** to review the **Calculated config** and the **Pending Changes** list of exact commands before committing to them.

:::caution
Applying changes saves them and reboots your flight controller straight away — there's no separate confirmation step. Double check the Pending Changes list before clicking Save and Reboot.
:::
